# Copywriting Frameworks

Persuasive copy frameworks, headline patterns, value propositions, and CTAs.

## Core Frameworks

### AIDA — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action
**Use:** Landing pages, ads, full sales pages
**Template:** "[Attention hook]. [Interesting detail]. [Desirable outcome]. [Clear CTA]."
**Example:** "Your team wastes 8 hours a week in status meetings. TaskFlow turns updates into one async feed. Ship faster without the calendar chaos. Start free →"

### PAS — Problem, Agitate, Solution
**Use:** Cold outreach, ads, pain-driven copy
**Template:** "[Problem]? [Agitate the cost]. [Solution] fixes it."
**Example:** "Still chasing invoices by email? Every late payment drains cash you already earned. Billed sends polite auto-reminders so you get paid on time."

### FAB — Feature, Advantage, Benefit
**Use:** Product pages, feature sections
**Template:** "[Feature] lets you [advantage], so you can [benefit]."
**Example:** "Offline mode keeps your notes synced without wifi, so you never lose an idea on a flight."

### BAB — Before, After, Bridge
**Use:** Transformation stories, case studies
**Template:** "[Painful before]. [Better after]. [Product] is the bridge."

### 4 Ps — Promise, Picture, Proof, Push
**Use:** Long-form sales, email
**Template:** "[Promise a result]. [Picture the outcome]. [Proof it works]. [Push to act now]."

### PASTOR — Problem, Amplify, Story, Transformation, Offer, Response
**Use:** Webinars, long sales letters

## Value Proposition Template

```
For [target customer]
who [need / pain],
[Product] is a [category]
that [key benefit].
Unlike [main alternative],
we [unique differentiator].
```

Keep the public-facing one-liner under 12 words: **"[Outcome] for [audience], without [pain]."**
Example: "Async standups for remote teams, without another meeting."

## Headline Patterns

| Pattern | Template | Example |
|---------|----------|---------|
| How-to | "How to [result] without [pain]" | "How to grow MRR without ad spend" |
| Number | "[N] ways to [achieve goal]" | "7 ways to cut churn this quarter" |
| Question | "Are you [making mistake]?" | "Are you leaving signups on the table?" |
| Contrast | "[Old way] is dead. Meet [new way]." | "Spreadsheets are dead. Meet live dashboards." |
| Outcome | "[Result] in [timeframe]" | "Launch a store in 10 minutes" |
| Curiosity | "The [adjective] reason [audience] [behavior]" | "The real reason great devs quit" |

### Power & sensory words
free, proven, instantly, effortless, guaranteed, hidden, exclusive, new, because, you.
Always favor **you** over **we**; lead with the reader's outcome.

## CTA Library

| Goal | Weak | Strong |
|------|------|--------|
| Signup | "Submit" | "Start free — no card" |
| Demo | "Contact us" | "See it in 5 minutes" |
| Purchase | "Buy now" | "Get [product] today" |
| Download | "Download" | "Get the free template" |
| Trial | "Sign up" | "Try free for 14 days" |

CTA rules: one primary action per screen, first-person voice tested ("Start my trial"), add a risk-reducer microcopy ("Cancel anytime").

## Voice & Tone Quick Guide

- Match brand voice first (load `brand` skill / `docs/brand-guidelines.md` if present).
- Read at a 6th–8th grade level; cut jargon and hedge words ("just", "really", "very").
- Use concrete numbers and specifics over adjectives.
- One idea per sentence; vary sentence length for rhythm.

## Self-Review Checklist

1. Is the **first line** a hook a busy reader can't ignore?
2. Is it about the **reader's outcome**, not your features?
3. Is there **one** clear CTA?
4. Is every claim **provable** (proof, number, or testimonial nearby)?
5. Could a competitor say the exact same thing? If yes, sharpen the differentiator.
6. Read aloud — does it sound human?
